<p>[中国人的孩子，有些被归类成中产阶级，这是个被发明出来的词汇。最后才发现，我们和所有人都一样。什么都想是，什么都不是。<br>
      I’m a Chinese guy, some of us are classified as middle class, a term invented by society. In the end, we realize that we are all the same. We want to be everything and none at all]</p>
    <p>I<br>
      I am astonished by the tidy minds of the middle class children,</p>
    <p>still handcuffed by disciplinarians behind milling machines</p>
    <p>posed like an airplane,</p>
    <p>bent over,</p>
    <p>body at a ninety-degree angle, arms thrown straight back.</p>
    <p>Their precision has reached its limit,</p>
    <p>some on medical parole, some out on bail, some on probation.</p>
    <p>Their parents have paid their dues in death and taxes, but</p>
    <p>the rights they held were missing in the beginning,</p>
    <p>and must be stripped away again,</p>
    <p>justified like Confuciusism</p>
    <p>They see  “knowing” and “knowledge” as the same</p>
    <p>No one needs to know.</p>
    <p>They are merely machines that can fuck.</p>
    <p>Nothing keeps them from becoming</p>
    <p>Laplace’s demon, omniscient and omnipotent,</p>
    <p>seeing every atom’s trajectory in the universe and declaring:</p>
    <p>“Its all fucking nothing.”</p>
    <p>Time is like pirated porn downloaded by countless hands,</p>
    <p>someone labels it “sister-in-law,”</p>
    <p>someone in Vietnam uploads it in Vietnamese,</p>
    <p>someone titles “college student,” someone “co-worker,”</p>
    <p>someone “friend’s wife”—top-tier MILF.</p>
    <p>They collide with these spawning endless questions.</p>
    <p>They have many rights - battered, awaiting delivery,</p>
    <p>parcels that sit at collection points;</p>
    <p>They all have great potential,</p>
    <p>all waiting for demolition and relocation,</p>
    <p>all indefinitely postponed,</p>
    <p>all applying for permits</p>
    <p>to raze their cocoon of information,</p>
    <p>their minds haunted by songs and memes</p>
    <p>made by humans with upgraded brains.</p>
    <p>They are surprised to find they can sing every shrill internet noise.</p>
    <p>Then, the Senior official proposed a toast,</p>
    <p>and the director toasts:</p>
    <p>“These dwellings will not hold you.”</p>
    <p>All rise,</p>
    <p>announce to society in line,</p>
    <p>with permits, dates, and prices clear.</p>
    <p>But it must rain. Don’t demolish yet.</p>
    <p>Sometimes in rain their forms shift.</p>
    <p>Sometimes face recognition fails.</p>
    <p>After rain, arthropods emerge everywhere.</p>
    <p>How much better can it be outside?</p>
    <p>Everywhere you go;</p>
    <p>scan your face, pay with your image, enter street blocks</p>
    <p>Their brown chitinous shells</p>
    <p>reflect only streetlight spots</p>
    <p>antennae extended,</p>
    <p>eight compound eyes fixed on cameras,</p>
    <p>yet they endure.</p>
    <p>Praised for survival and adaptability.</p>
    <p>They are not colonial vanguards nor Young Pioneers</p>
    <p>nor construction brigades nor sanitation crews.</p>
    <p>They are just very weary.</p>
    <p>Later, the Hebei Provincial Department of Water Resources</p>
    <p>and the Hebei Provincial Meteorological Bureau</p>
    <p>jointly issue a disaster warning.</p>
    <p>After the rain, they crawl across the mountains and plains.</p>
    <p>Dreams writhe from burrows and grass heaps.</p>
    <p>They search with their asses in the air, holding flashlights.</p>
    <p>Some collect dreams that sell for one yuan each.</p>
    <p>They seize them with all their might.</p>
    <p>Once compensation standards and resettlement plans are set,</p>
    <p>they eagerly begin to be packed into suitcases, wearing anxious looks.</p>
    <p>They don't open the sunroof or the windows.</p>
    <p>They don't let any smoke escape the car.</p>
    <p>They adhere to vigorous industrial development feeding agriculture,</p>
    <p>combined with environmentalism and sustainability.</p>
    <p>See those great smokestacks,</p>
    <p>factories and workshops.</p>
    <p>A cloud of inert gas hangs over their heads.</p>
    <p>They use border collies to herd cattle,</p>
    <p>driving the gas into sunscreen bottles,</p>
    <p>preparing to ship and sell.</p>
    <p>They list these sunscreens on second-hand trading platforms.</p>
    <p>This is this month's quota.</p>
    <p>This stuff matters.</p>
    <p>They buy it, spray themselves, stand outside.</p>
    <p>This is the job they've been assigned,</p>
    <p>bearing UV burden for humanity.</p>
    <p>This method avoids  the friction of unemployment.</p>
    <p>Cyclical careers.</p>
    <p>As long as the sun is shining.</p>
    <p>They hold bright rice bowls. Wear anti-scalding gloves.</p>
    <p>Later they all go abroad,</p>
    <p>applying for visas, Fujian excluded.</p>
    <p>They trade in ideology.</p>
    <p>They learn of their naivety,</p>
    <p>Then they learn what in themselves can be sold.</p>
    <p>There they search every pocket,</p>
    <p>regretting they sold all those dreams they caught.</p>
    <p>Where dreams exist, big money can be made; what else remains?</p>
    <p>Sell ideology, earn money,; also free their left hand.</p>
    <p>Others among overseas Chinese curse them,</p>
    <p>calling them traitors.</p>
    <p>They do not blame them—they have lived little.</p>
    <p>They say if the nation faces disaster they all donate.</p>
    <p>Yet they look like the kind of men who let the girl pay for the hotel room,</p>
    <p>and pocket the deposit on their way out.</p>
    <p>They aren't bad at heart:</p>
    <p>The rise and fall of the nation</p>
    <p>is the concern of every common man.</p>
    <p>Illegal and temporary structures</p>
    <p>will not be compensated.</p>
    <p>Those who lived lawfully</p>
    <p>If they sign,here can be compensation for demolition.</p>
    <p>Holding the money, not knowing which city to go to.</p>
    <p>They continue to learn,</p>
    <p>to seek knowing or knowledge,</p>
    <p>more and more,Masters and PhDs,</p>
    <p>they'll earn millions in the future.</p>
    <p>Back then they signed the papers,</p>
    <p>unless their new apartment building is also left unfinished.</p>
    <p>This new house feels like an interrogation room.</p>
    <p>Why is it like this?</p>
    <p>they ask.</p>
    <p>Free to ask: yes, but must first register.</p>
    <p>Register: yes, but must fill forms.</p>
    <p>Forms: yes, but must answer questions:</p>
    <p>What stance does your speech represent?<br>
      Do you understand its implications?<br>
      Are you aware of its gist?<br>
      Do you grasp its thought?<br>
      Do you perceive its reflections?<br>
      Do you support its stance?<br>
      Do you participate in discussion?<br>
      Do you hold a clear attitude toward related statements?<br>
      How do you define the problem?</p>
    <p>What are your views on this?<br>
      Are your views based on speculation?<br>
      Are they grounded in evidence?<br>
      Do you have solid data supporting your argument?<br>
      Are your views aligned with collective values?</p>
    <h2 class="part">II</h2>
    <p>A bottle of Brave the World is eight yuan, he said.</p>
    <p>He finished it, swapped a second for a Snow, five yuan.</p>
    <p>He said Tianya was sold out, then fetched two bottles of Big Green Stick.</p>
    <p>I can see the fridge full of Tianya.</p>
    <p>I can see his literary flair, writing with flowing grace about the</p>
    <p>ignorant middle class children,</p>
    <p>claiming impotence to rebel against the world.</p>
    <p>He just wants to save a few yuan.</p>
    <p>I know what that feels like.</p>
    <p>Security checks are unusually strict, yet someone still makes noise in the silent carriage.</p>
    <p>Society needs a third child; you need sleep; children are louder.</p>
    <p>I can see these children, powerless before the world,</p>
    <p>yet treated humanely by the greatest slave-owner.</p>
    <p>We loathe our former selves, inward and outward.</p>
    <p>Sour scent; rotten condoms in ashtrays;</p>
    <p>nothing but air-conditioning. A smell like dead children.</p>
    <p>I know what that feels like.</p>
    <p>I can see the clumsy Beatniks on the internet sharing their sex lives,</p>
    <p>Finding niche topics to substitute talent.</p>
    <p>Best not to do it on a rooftop,</p>
    <p>Lest you run into a pioneer exactly like yourself,</p>
    <p>making things pretty awkward.</p>
    <p>Imperialist devils swarm over the mountains, coming again and again, planes whistling, only one platoon left on our position, defend the hilltop to the death.</p>
    <p>I can see the dandruff flying from dense rows of hair on the subway, in the mall, in the park,</p>
    <p>people look at me but strafing their gazes.</p>
    <p>I recoil.</p>
    <p>Want to dive into a trench, I need supplies.</p>
    <p>And warriors standing alone against hundreds still envision the future before death,</p>
    <p>and modernity.</p>
    <p>I don't know what that feels like.</p>
    <p>Walking campus, reading the finest “-isms.”</p>
    <p>Weekends finishing drafts; house-mates all writing novels,</p>
    <p>going on dates armed with literary magazines,</p>
    <p>recalling the beloved twentieth century’s eighties.</p>
    <p>I want to know what that feels like.</p>
    <p>I can see Chinese populists debating the future of the world. I try to return to simplicity,</p>
    <p>treading shallow, then deep</p>
    <p>on piles of flamboyant trash, looking down on most people.</p>
    <p>And yet "culture is of the masses, and therefore democratic."</p>
    <p>I don't know what that feels like.</p>
    <p>I don't know what that feels like.</p>
    <p>I only see what I want to see. I only know what I already know. I only like what I’m good at. I only resent what I resist. I only support what I endorse. I only indulge in passion. I only crave what I aspire to.</p>
    <p>I feel awake, yet spiritless.</p>
    <p>I am both colloquial and taciturn,</p>
    <p>I read hate as unrequited love,</p>
    <p>I brandish indulgence as anti-Confucian revolt.</p>
    <p>I classify extreme weather as ideological issue,</p>
    <p>I interpret madness as social skill.</p>
    <p>I vanish in intimacy, knowing only avoidance.</p>
    <p>I hold objectivity with humanistic care,</p>
    <p>I attack ugliness while seeking beauty,</p>
    <p>I am the moralist embracing change,</p>
    <p>I am the self-admiring plagiarist,</p>
    <p>I am the smog of holiday resorts.</p>
    <p>I want to live this way.</p>
    <p>Even aliens cannot stop me,</p>
    <p>until I no longer recognise the character “I.”</p>
    <p>Is it I who knows myself better,</p>
    <p>or you who understands yourselves better?</p>
    <p>I know what that feels like.</p>
    <p>Because we are all ignorant middle class children of this generation.</p>
