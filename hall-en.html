About the Author

Someone from Qinhuangdao

◎

Qinhuangdao · 39.9° N, 119.6° E

Sometimes I try to describe a feeling—countless skeletons; writers with hollow chests, clutching close their only private manuscripts, already weathered to ash. They themselves would never dare believe they might be remembered.

Thinking of this, I want to go home at once. I want to turn on my computer and carefully create a new folder—this time, not on the desktop. I will carefully give it an important name, something other than New Folder. I no longer trust distant cloud servers to keep my information. I no longer trust dumb computers. I know I will spill water on mine.

I set down their sentences and mine with care. Nothing special. Yes, I want to go home at once, even though I am already home. I only want, for a while, to fill myself with the desire to explore and to know, until I reach the edge of my comfort zone or the limits of what I know; after searching a few keywords, the past will abruptly return to me, and I will hang my head and go on inventing.

In Invisible Cities, Italo Calvino tells us to “seek and learn to recognize who and what, in the midst of the inferno, are not inferno, then make them endure, give them space.” I try to knead together the signs carried by people I once knew, shaping copies of them.

I think of the long, garrulous, lyrical letters that recur in modernist novels. There is no longer room for such things in people's minds. I hope there might still be room for these.
